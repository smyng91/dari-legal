Dari Privacy Policy

Effective 2026-11-21 · Version 6

Dari is a chat app made by Sam Yang (“we”). This policy explains what happens to your information when you use it.

In short: Dari has no servers and no user accounts. Your chats stay on your device. What you send to an AI model goes straight from your device to the service you chose, using your own API key. We do not receive it.

1. Who we are

Sam Yang is responsible for the app. For privacy questions, requests or complaints, write to smyng91@gmail.com. We are also the privacy officer for the app.

2. What stays on your device

  • Your chats: messages, photos and files you attach (PDFs, documents, spreadsheets, text), images the app makes for you, and the web sources and places shown with an answer.
  • Your topics (their instructions and knowledge), your long-term memories, your settings and the usage statistics.
  • Your API keys, in the system’s secure storage (the iOS Keychain or Android Keystore).
  • Sign-ins for the accounts you connect (Settings → Accounts), in the same secure storage, or for Google, in Google’s own sign-in software on your device.

We cannot see any of it. It is not included in iCloud, Google or computer backups, and it does not move to a new device.

3. What leaves your device, and to whom

Dari sends data to a service only when you use a feature that needs it, only to services whose API key you added (or, for Apple Maps, that you turned on), and always over an encrypted connection (HTTPS):

  • Anthropic (Claude), based in the United States. When a Claude model answers, picks a route, or writes a summary or memory: your message, the recent conversation and its summary, photos and files you attach, the topic’s instructions and knowledge, your long-term memories, the web or place results for the message, and your device’s current date, time and time zone (so it can work out dates such as “tomorrow”). Privacy policy: https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy
  • OpenAI (GPT and image generation), based in the United States. When a GPT model is used: the same as for Claude. When you make or edit an image: your request and the images to edit. Dari asks OpenAI not to store conversations (store: false). Privacy policy: https://openai.com/policies/privacy-policy/
  • DeepSeek, based in the People’s Republic of China. When a DeepSeek model is used: the same as for Claude. DeepSeek’s privacy policy says it stores data in the People’s Republic of China. Privacy policy: https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-privacy-policy.html
  • Google (Gemini), based in the United States. When a Gemini model is used: the same as for Claude. With a free Gemini API key (no billing), Google’s terms let it use what is sent to improve its products, and human reviewers may read it. With billing turned on for the key’s Google Cloud project, Google says it does not use prompts or answers for that, and keeps them only for a limited time to detect abuse. Privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • Tavily (web search), based in the United States. When a model searches the web through Tavily: the search query, written from your message. Privacy policy: https://www.tavily.com/privacy
  • Google (Maps and places), based in the United States. When a model searches for places: the place search, written from your message; and place IDs, to load details and photos. With nearby places turned on, your approximate location (rounded to about 1 km). Google may use your IP address to show places near you. Privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • Apple (Apple Maps), based in the United States. Only on iPhone, when you turn on Apple Maps for places and have no Google Maps key: the place search, written from your message; Apple Place IDs, to show the places again; and requests for Look Around pictures of the places shown. With nearby places turned on, your approximate location (rounded to about 1 km). Privacy policy: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/

Because you use your own API key, you are the customer of each service, and its privacy policy and data retention apply to what it receives. We do not get copies. A service may process data in countries other than the one it is based in, as its policy describes.

If you set your own server address in Settings → Advanced, requests go to that server instead, and its operator (usually you) decides what happens to them.

Your choice. Adding a key is your permission for Dari to send data to that service; the app explains what will be sent before you save it. To stop, remove the key in Settings → API keys. Apple Maps is used only after you allow it, and you can turn it off in Settings → Places. Features that need the service then stop working.

Connected accounts. If you connect Gmail and Google Calendar, Outlook mail and calendar, or Microsoft Teams in Settings → Accounts, Dari signs in to Google or Microsoft from your device and keeps the sign-in in the system’s secure storage. When you ask about your mail, calendar or chats, the AI model answering can have the app search and read them: the app gets the messages, events or chat messages from Google or Microsoft and sends what was read, as part of the conversation, to the AI service answering that message. DeepSeek can’t read your accounts, but text from them that is already in a chat goes with that chat to whichever service answers later. Emails, events and Teams messages are sent only when you tap Send on a card that shows exactly what will be sent. We never receive any of it. Dari’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements: it is used only to answer your requests in the app, never for advertising and never to train AI models. What an AI service may do with what it receives is set by its own terms (some free API plans let the service use it to improve its models). To stop, disconnect the account in Settings → Accounts; you can also remove the access in your Google or Microsoft account settings.

4. Reports you send us

If you report an answer, Dari sends us the answer’s text, the reason and note you give, the model’s name, the app version and the platform (iOS or Android). Your question is included only if you choose. We use reports to find and reduce harmful answers. We keep them for up to 12 months and delete them sooner if you ask. If a report is sent by email instead, we also see your email address.

5. What we don’t do

  • No analytics, advertising, tracking or crash reporting, and no selling or sharing of your data for advertising.
  • The app reads your location only if you turn on “Use my location for nearby places” in Settings → Places and allow it. It then asks the system for your approximate location, rounds it to about 1 km, and sends it only with place searches. You can turn it off there or in the system settings. Map services may also estimate an area from your IP address.
  • When an answer is about products to buy, your device opens the shop and review pages the web search returned, and later the product pictures, straight from those websites, to show product cards with prices and ratings. Like any web page you visit, those sites see your IP address and the page asked for; the app sends them nothing from your chats.
  • The camera and photos are used only when you take or pick a photo. Location data in photos (EXIF) is removed before they are stored or sent.
  • You can edit a suggested event, contact, call, email or message before opening another app. If you allow Dari to open apps for you (it asks the first time an answer suggests an action, and you can change this in Settings → Device actions), the destination app opens with the suggested details as soon as the answer arrives. If you choose a recipient in the system contact picker, Dari receives only the phone number or email address you select. It stays in the temporary draft and is excluded from chat history and AI requests. When the destination app opens, it receives the details in the draft and its privacy policy applies. You confirm sending, saving or calling there. For these, Dari does not request access to the phone’s address book, calendar or messages; it reads an inbox or a calendar only through the accounts you connect (section 3). Android and iOS 17 or later can open the calendar editor directly. Earlier iOS versions offer to share the event details.
  • Files are read only when you pick them in the system file picker. Their text is read on your device; a PDF is sent as it is, and other files as their text, to the AI service that answers that message. A file is sent again with each later message in the chat, so the model can keep using it, until newer files take its place or it moves out of the conversation the model sees.

6. How long data is kept, and how to delete it

  • On your device: until you delete it. You can delete a message (press and hold it), a chat, a topic or a memory, all chats at once in Settings → Storage → Delete all chats, and each API key in Settings → API keys. Uninstalling the app removes its data. On iOS, the system keeps API keys in the Keychain after an uninstall, where no other app can read them; Dari erases them if it is installed again. To remove them right away, delete them in Settings → API keys before uninstalling. To disconnect an account, use Settings → Accounts.
  • At the services: as their privacy policies describe. To ask a service about data it holds, contact it through the links above.
  • Reports: up to 12 months, as described in section 4.

7. Security

Connections are encrypted. API keys are kept in the system’s secure storage and never written to files or logs. App files are excluded from backups and, on iOS, encrypted while the device is locked. Deleted messages are overwritten. No method is perfectly secure: on a rooted or jailbroken device, other software may be able to read app data.

8. Children

Dari is not meant for anyone under 18, and we do not knowingly receive information from children.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR, UK GDPR, the California Consumer Privacy Act or Korea’s Personal Information Protection Act), you may have the right to access, correct or delete your information, to object to or restrict its use, and to complain to a data protection authority. We hold no information about you apart from reports you send, so you can exercise most of these rights in the app itself. For reports, write to us. For data held by a service, contact that service.

10. Transfers to other countries

When you use a service, your data travels from your device to that service at the moment you send it. Anthropic, OpenAI, Tavily, Google and Apple are based in the United States; DeepSeek is based in the People’s Republic of China and says it stores data there. Each service may also process data in other countries, as its policy describes. The purpose is to answer your request, and each service keeps the data as its own policy says. You can refuse a transfer by not adding that service’s key, or by removing it.

11. Changes

When we change this policy, we update the date and version above. For changes that matter to you, the app asks you to read and accept the policy again.
